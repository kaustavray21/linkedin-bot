Why are some paintings worth millions of dollars?

I just walked out of the National Gallery here in London…
and I saw paintings worth like… tens of millions.

And I’m just standing there thinking…
the paint on that canvas is probably like $100.

So what makes it worth Millions?

It’s not the paint.
It’s not even the skill.

There are insanely talented artists
who will never sell a painting for more than $500.

What actually makes it expensive is the frame around it.

The name.
The story.
The context.
The way it’s positioned.

And I had this realization…
this is exactly what’s happening in business.

There are people charging $500/month…
and there are people charging $10,000/month…

for almost the same service.
Same skill.
Same outcome.

The difference?
Not talent.
Not effort.

They just know how to frame what they do.

So if you’re stuck at low-ticket right now…
it’s probably not your skill.
It’s how you’re packaging it.

1. Who it's for [Niche down the pain]
Generic offer = commodity price.
Specific offer = premium price
"I run ads" vs "I help D2C skincare brands get their first 500 customers using Meta ads"
The more specific the pain you name, the more the right person feels seen
Niche doesn't limit you, it qualifies your buyers and kills price objections

2. How big the pain is [Pain amplification]
Clients don't pay for services. They pay for relief
Sell the cost of NOT fixing it, not just the benefit of fixing it
What is your client losing every month this problem exists? Revenue, time, opportunity, make it concrete
The offer that names the pain better than the client can = instant trust

3. Perceived value [The packaging]
Price is a signal. Low price creates doubt, not trust
Four things that build perceived value: Specificity of the promise
Confidence in delivery
Proof, even one strong case study changes everything
Structure, how you present it (deck vs verbal vs PDF signals completely different levels)
Your guarantee, risk reversal is the single biggest lever on price
