My post went super viral on Linkedin (150K + impressions) and it backfired on me

 
(No, I didn't get cancelled. Calm down.)
 
It's actually one of the best-performing pieces of content I've put out. 4,317 like.
 
Going viral is like winning the lottery and then realizing you owe taxes on it
 
Infact we don't make money when a post goes viral. 
We make money when a post flops. 
 
Every time you go viral, everyone sees your post the ones who'll buy from you, and the ones who never will. Usually it's an 80/20 split: 
80% scrollers, 20% actual buyers.
 
Barry Schwartz called this the paradox of choice. More options, more paralysis, less satisfaction.
 
Social media for business is a trust-building funnel slow and steady. 
Sudden traffic doesn't skip that line. 
It just adds another filter you now have to sort through manually.
 
Yes, virality builds credibility, especially if you're in the content game. 
But it leaves scars too : DMs at 2am from people who like, comment, and then ghost you!
 
Virality is great for the ego but so so rough on the inbox.
 
So next time your content blows up and you're feeling amazing about it, stop. 
Look at the stats. 
 
Ask yourself: did this bring you people? Or did it bring you the right people?
 
Because growth was never about how many eyes are on you. 
It's about how many of the right eyes are.
